A posted date is often a repost date

Employers often repost roles that are still open, and each repost gives the posting a new date. Of the 3,949 roles dated Sep 26 to Oct 2, 773 first appeared that week. Another 904 had first been seen less than a month earlier, 702 had been up for 90 days or more, and 183 for more than half a year.

If you are going

A role with this week's date may have been open for months, so ask how long it has been open and where the search stands. New roles come down fast: across these hosts, 42% of the postings that closed in the past year were up for two weeks or less, and the average was 30 days. The daily lists mark the roles that are new, for example Monday's new roles.

How we measured it

Roles come from the Metix AI Platform, which aggregates public job postings from many sources: open roles dated Sep 26 to Oct 2, pulled Oct 3, in Bay Area cities or listed as US remote, at companies hosting events on the official SF Tech Week calendar from October 5 to 11. Companies are counted as they are named on their postings. The same title at the same company is counted once.

New means the role first appeared in the Metix AI Platform's job history inside that week. New roles reach the history within a day of their posted date, so a role counted as a repost was not just found late. LinkedIn job ids also grow over time, and they agree: none of the 361 roles at NVIDIA, 158 at Google, 89 at Adobe or 56 at Databricks carries an id as recent as that week's new roles. The history goes back about a year, so the oldest reposts may be older than shown.

Time up is the time between the first and last day a posting was seen, for 601,069 US postings at these companies that came down between November 2025 and September 20, 2026. Gaps in collection can stretch it, and it is not time to hire.

Not included yet: week-over-week hiring pace. We cannot yet separate real changes from changes in how often each company's postings are collected.
